Easter weekend, April 2015. Inside Hatton Garden Safe Deposit, a drill grinds through fifty centimeters of reinforced concrete. At 00:21 an alarm trips. Security calls police. Police don't come. Over the next 72 hours, a crew works undisturbed, cracking open 73 of 999 boxes. The haul: somewhere between 14 million and 200 million pounds in gold, gems, and cash — nobody agrees on the number, because nobody knows what was really inside those boxes. The gang isn't young. Ringleader Brian Reader is 76. His crew average age: 66. Old men with crowbars and a diamond-tipped drill, caught weeks later because they drove real cars with real license plates straight past CCTV, then bragged in a bugged pub called The Castle. Nine arrests. Convictions follow fast. Case closed — except most of the loot, an estimated ten million pounds worth, was never recovered. And one man who was inside that vault, caught on grainy footage, never surfaced. Police nicknamed him Basil. No name. No face. No arrest. Ten years later, Basil has never been identified.
