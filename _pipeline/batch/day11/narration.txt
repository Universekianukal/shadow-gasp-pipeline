September 29, 1982. Mary Kellerman, 12, dies within hours of taking Tylenol for a cold. Elmhurst, Illinois. Same day, Adam Janus, 27, collapses in his kitchen. Dead by 3pm. His family gathers at his house to grieve — and takes pills from the same bottle. His brother Stanley, 25, and Stanley's wife Theresa, 19, die within the day. Three from one bottle. By September 30, three more dead across the Chicago suburbs. Seven total. All Extra-Strength Tylenol. All laced with potassium cyanide — doses over ten thousand times what's needed to kill. Investigators expect factory contamination. They're wrong. The bottles came from different plants, different lots, sold at six separate stores miles apart. Someone bought bottles off shelves, injected cyanide into capsules, and put them back — before anyone noticed. A man named James Lewis sends Johnson & Johnson a letter: pay one million dollars or the killing continues. He's caught, convicted of extortion, serves thirteen years. Never charged with the murders. No one ever is. The bottles sit in a Cook County evidence locker to this day. Seven names. Zero convictions. Forty-two years later, whoever stood in those aisles has never been identified.
