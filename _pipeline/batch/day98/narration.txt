January 27, 1968. The French submarine Minerve surfaces briefly in the Mediterranean to radio Toulon — she will be back by 1:00 PM. 52 men aboard. She dives. She is never heard from again. The response is immediate. Sixty vessels, aircraft from four nations, acoustic sweeps across 4,000 square kilometers of seafloor. For two months they search. They find nothing. No debris. No oil slick. No bodies. A 780-ton submarine has simply ceased to exist. For 51 years the Minerve is a ghost. France launches three separate deep-water surveys. Each fails. Then in July 2019, a private survey vessel using autonomous underwater drones locates the wreck — upright on the seabed at 2,370 meters, 28 miles off Toulon. Intact. No explosion. No visible collision damage. But here is what haunts every investigator. The hull is whole. Something sent 52 men to the bottom in minutes and left no wound on the submarine itself. French naval analysts list seven possible causes — a snort-valve flood, a rudder jam, an explosion. They cannot eliminate a single one. The answer is still inside that hull, on a seafloor too deep for any human diver to ever reach.
