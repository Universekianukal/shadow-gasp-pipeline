July 16, 1952. A ten-year-old girl walks away from Camp Sloane in Salisbury, Connecticut, and is never seen again. Connie Smith, granddaughter of a former Wyoming governor, had argued with another camper that morning. She left camp on foot, heading toward Lakeville, four miles south, to call her grandmother. Multiple witnesses saw her on the road. A motorist spotted her walking. A gas station attendant watched her pass. She was seen near a crossroads, still walking, still alone. Then nothing. The camp didn't report her missing for hours. They assumed she'd come back. By the time searchers mobilized, darkness had fallen. What followed was one of the largest manhunts in Connecticut history. Over a thousand volunteers. State police. Bloodhounds. The FBI. They searched 200 square miles of dense New England forest. They found nothing. No body. No clothing. No tracks beyond the road. A single shoe print near a creek bed was never confirmed as hers. Connie Smith walked a public road in broad daylight, was seen by at least three separate witnesses, and vanished between sightings less than a mile apart. Seventy-three years later, no trace has ever been found.
