Eight bodies. One axe. And a killer who didn't run. June 10th, 1912, Villisca, Iowa. A neighbor knocks on the Moore family's door at seven a.m. No answer. She lets herself in — and finds all four children dead in their beds. Then the parents. Then two neighbor girls sleeping over, ages eight and twelve. Eight victims, killed sometime between midnight and five a.m., all struck while they slept. The weapon: Josiah Moore's own axe, taken from his shed, found leaning in a corner, wiped clean. Here's what still unsettles investigators. Every mirror in the house is covered with clothing. Every victim's face is covered with bedding. A kerosene lamp burns dry at the foot of one bed — its glass chimney missing. On the stove, two pounds of raw bacon, untouched, hours old. Someone stayed in that house long after the killing was done. Iowa offers a $2,000 reward. A traveling minister is tried twice — one hung jury, one acquittal. A state senator hires detectives to frame a rival, then finds himself investigated. No one is ever convicted. The axe still exists, in a case, in a museum. What was the killer doing in that house until dawn?
