November 7th, 2000. 9:32 AM. A JCB excavator smashes through the perimeter fence of London's Millennium Dome at nearly 30 miles an hour. Inside: the Millennium Star, a 203-carat diamond worth £200 million, surrounded by eleven flawless blue diamonds. Total target: £350 million. Four men pour out in gas masks, throw smoke grenades, and attack the reinforced case with sledgehammers and a nail gun. They believe they have ninety seconds before armed response arrives. What they don't know: the cleaners mopping the floor thirty feet away are Flying Squad officers. Police have watched this crew for months, since a failed attempt back in February. And the diamond in that case isn't real — the genuine Millennium Star was swapped for a replica weeks earlier and moved to a vault across town. The nail gun fires into bulletproof glass. It doesn't give. Then thirty armed officers rise out of hiding — cleaners, tourists, staff — and the gang hits the ground before the smoke clears. A getaway speedboat idles on the Thames; its driver is arrested mid-throttle. Every man is caught. But no one has ever named who ordered a £200 million diamond, sight unseen, no questions asked. That buyer has never surfaced.
