July 26, 1974. A twelve-year-old girl cuts through the dunes at Race Point, Provincetown, and stops dead. A woman lies face-down in the sand. Nude. Hands resting behind her head. Nearly decapitated. She had long auburn hair. Manicured nails. An athletic build — five-foot-eight, about 145 pounds. No clothes, no purse, no identification. Nothing. The wound was so severe that investigators first thought she'd been struck by a vehicle. But the blow was deliberate. She'd been killed somewhere else and posed here, half a mile from the nearest road. For 49 years, she had no name. Detectives chased over 2,000 leads. A witness recalled seeing a woman matching her description near a blue sedan. Another claimed she'd checked into a local motel. Both leads went cold. Then in October 2023, genealogical DNA cracked it. She was Ruth Marie Terry, 37, from Tennessee. The twist: investigators named her husband, Guy Muldavin, as the killer. Muldavin had died in 2002 — 21 years before Ruth was identified. He never faced a single charge. And Muldavin had been linked to at least two other women who vanished without a trace. Ruth was buried in Provincetown under a headstone that read 'Unidentified Female.' She lay there for half a century, three miles from where tourists sunbathed.
