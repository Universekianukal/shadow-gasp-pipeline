Twenty-four-year-old Amy Wroe Bechtel leaves the Lander gym on July 24, 1995, to scout a 10K race course up the Loop Road. Her 1990 white Toyota Celica is found two days later near the trailhead, keys in the ignition, wallet inside. She’s never seen again. Search teams scour a 25-mile grid of the rugged Wind River Range. The case goes cold for two years. Then, a letter. Convicted serial killer Dale Wayne Eaton confesses to her murder, giving authorities specific details they never released. He claims he spotted her running, abducted her, and buried her body along the Sweetwater River. His location description is unnervingly precise. Investigators excavate multiple sites over four days in 1997. They find nothing. No evidence. No body. Eaton recants, his confession labeled a hoax for media attention. But the physical detail nagged at investigators. How did a prisoner in Rawlins, who’d never been to the specific area, know the exact terrain? The case officially remains an active missing persons file. Amy’s running shoes are still on the passenger seat of her impounded Celica. Her family waits for the one clue that will point to where the map ends.
