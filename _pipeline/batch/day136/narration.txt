Burbank, California. The morning of January 17, 1994. A violent jolt rips the San Fernando Valley awake. The Northridge earthquake. 6.7 magnitude. When the shaking stops, city officials at the Burbank Civic Center discover something bizarre. Not just a crack in the foundation. A clean, man-sized hole has been precision-cut into the cornerstone of their 1939 Art Deco building. The 1939 time capsule is gone. Police confirm it: the theft occurred sometime during the chaotic, ground-shaking 60-90 seconds of the main earthquake. Someone knew exactly where the lead-lined box was encased. They knew the exact tools needed to breach the thick granite. They executed the heist in the precise, terrifying window when alarms were dead, systems were down, and every eye was on the sky. Not a single security camera captured the moment. The theft became a secondary mystery buried in the rubble of a massive disaster. But here's the twist. The city never publicly confirmed the capsule’s contents. No manifest existed. Was it just ceremonial coins and papers? Or was there something more valuable—or dangerous—hidden in 1939 Burbank? An opportunistic thief, or a professional extraction? The earthquake that exposed the theft also erased its trail. The capsule has never been seen again.
