April 21, 2011. Police jackhammer through a concrete terrace in Nantes, France. Beneath it: five bodies. A mother and her four children, wrapped in sheets, buried in quicklime. The father — gone. For two weeks, nobody suspected a thing. Neighbors received letters: the family was relocating to Australia for a business opportunity. The children were withdrawn from school. Thank-you notes were sent to teachers. Meals were ordered for a farewell gathering. Every detail was fabricated. Between April 3 and 5, Xavier Dupont de Ligonnès drugged his family with sleeping pills, then shot each one with a .22 caliber rifle. Two dogs. Five people. He poured concrete over the grave and drove 900 kilometers south. Last confirmed sighting: April 15, a hotel parking lot in Roquebrune-sur-Argens. His car: abandoned. His laptop, his rifle, his phone — all left behind. What he took: nothing traceable. Over 13 years, French police logged over 900 sightings. A monk in a monastery. A homeless man in Marseille. A suicide in Italy. DNA tested every time. Zero matches. Xavier Dupont de Ligonnès vanished so thoroughly that investigators still argue over the only question that matters: is he still alive?
