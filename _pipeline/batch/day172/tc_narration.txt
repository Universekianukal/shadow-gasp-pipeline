November 26, 1983. Six men cut through a Heathrow warehouse roof. They douse guards in gasoline, threaten to burn them alive. The target: the Brinks Mat vault. They expect £3 million in cash. Instead, 6,800 gold bars gleam back. £26 million. Pure 24-karat bullion. 3.8 tons of impossible wealth. The heist lasts 90 minutes. But the gold is poison. Too hot to move. They need to melt it, launder it through shady deals across Europe. Police catch a break: a robber brags in a pub. Arrests follow. Yet the masterminds slip away. £20 million vanishes. Bodies pile up. Informants shot, witnesses disappeared. Launderers like Kenneth Noye walk free after trials. Decades on, most gold is still missing. Buried? Smuggled? Spent on mansions? The truth lies in a web of corruption, with keys held by the dead.
