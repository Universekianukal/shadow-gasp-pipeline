Austin, Texas. December 6, 1991. Four teenage girls close the "I Can't Believe It's Yogurt!" shop. The front door locks at 11:45 PM. Inside, Amy and Sarah work in the back. Jennifer and Eliza clean the front. Ten minutes later, a 911 call: a frantic voice reports a shooting. Police arrive to find smoke. Firefighters discover the horror. The four girls are in the rear freezer. Bound. Shot execution-style. The store is set ablaze. It becomes Austin's largest-ever investigation. Over 800 people interviewed. Two years go by. Nothing. Then, a break. A robbery suspect confesses. He names four accomplices. One man provides a detailed confession. He describes the bindings, the shootings, the fire. The case appears solved. Then, the story fractures. The suspect recants. He claims coercion. Physical evidence fails to match. The airtight case develops holes. By 1999, all charges are dropped. The killers remain free. In 2002, DNA evidence emerges. A male profile, unknown, from the scene. In 2009, a second profile is found. They don't match the confessed men. They don't match each other. Two separate, unidentified men. The case technically remains open, but the leads are ice cold. The four girls are still waiting.
