February 25th, 1957. A college student cuts through a patch of woods in Fox Chase, Philadelphia, and finds a bassinet box half-buried in weeds, forty feet off the road. Inside: a boy, maybe four to six years old. Naked. Malnourished. His hair had been cut after he died. No one reports him missing. Not that week. Not that year. Detectives fingerprint over 1,200 children in state care. Nothing matches. For decades, his photo stays taped to precinct walls. His body gets embalmed twice, kept viewable, in case someone finally recognizes him. Sixty-five years pass. No name. Then, in December 2022, forensic genealogists pull DNA from his bones and trace it through a family tree. His name: Joseph Augustus Zarelli. A name goes on a headstone at last. But naming him doesn't answer who put him in that box. Who cut his hair. Who let him starve, and for how long. His birth mother's identity is still disputed. No one has ever been charged. The road by the woods is gone. The case, officially, is still open.
