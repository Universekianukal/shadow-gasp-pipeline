Three kids leave home with four shillings between them. January 26th, 1966. Australia Day, Adelaide. Jane, 9. Arnna, 7. Grant, 4. They catch the bus alone to Glenelg Beach, half a mile from home — normal, for the time. Their mother expects them on the noon bus. Then the two o'clock. Neither comes.

A postman later says he saw them that morning with a tall, blond stranger, mid-thirties, deeply tanned. Around lunchtime, a baker sells the children meat pies — paid for with a crisp five-pound note. They'd left the house with shillings. By three, witnesses place them walking toward the dunes with the same man. They are never seen again.

The search becomes the largest in South Australian history. Nothing. Years pass. Letters arrive claiming to be from Jane — handwriting experts split down the middle, never agreeing.

In 2013, a tip sends police tearing up a factory floor in North Plympton, chasing a suspect who'd already been dead for nine years. Months of digging. Concrete. Drains. Soil samples. No bodies. No answers.

Fifty-eight years on, nobody has explained where that five-pound note came from.
