October 8th, 1949. A woman walking through Griffith Park finds a purse lying in the weeds, its strap violently torn. Inside: a note, torn off mid-sentence. The night before, Jean Spangler, 27, a Hollywood actress and single mother, left her apartment on South Colgate Avenue at 5 p.m. She told her sister-in-law two things — she was meeting her ex-husband about child support, then heading to a night film shoot. She never said which studio. She never came home. The note read: 'Kirk, can't wait any longer, Gordon told me to be sure to see Dr. Scott. It will work best while mother is away.' Then — torn. The rest, gone. Detectives circle one name: Kirk Douglas, filming in Los Angeles that same week. He denies ever meeting her. Police hunt for 'Dr. Scott,' a name whispered through an illegal abortion ring tied to the studios. He's never found. Neither is Gordon. No body. No arrest. No film shoot she was ever booked for. Seventy-five years later, the missing half of that note has never surfaced — and neither has she.
