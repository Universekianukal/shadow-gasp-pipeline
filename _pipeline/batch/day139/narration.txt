St. Louis County, June 1999. Ricky McCormick’s body decomposes in a field for ten days before it’s found. The 41-year-old man with a fourth-grade education left no obvious cause of death. But in his pants pockets, investigators find two notes. Both are crammed with a bizarre, handwritten code. Strange strings of letters, numbers, and symbols like `FBRC,` `NLR,` `MOC,` and `AAT.` The FBI’s Cryptanalysis Unit takes the case. They use supercomputers, frequency analysis, every tool in their arsenal. The patterns don’t fit standard ciphers. They don’t match any known language. After twelve years, in 2011, the FBI makes a public appeal for help, posting the full cipher online. Thousands of amateur sleuths and professional cryptographers try to crack it. The code resists them all. The forensic pathologist later states the death could be natural, or homicide, or accident: undetermined. The unbreakable notes are not a solution; they’re the mystery itself. Who was Ricky McCormick? And what secret did he carry, that died with him in that field?
