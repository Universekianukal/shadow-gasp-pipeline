1975 — a semi-truck pulling a refrigerated trailer parks for the night outside a potato processing plant in Blackfoot, Idaho. Inside are 100,000 pounds of Russet Burbanks. Market value: $25,000. By morning, the trailer is gone — tractor, driver, and spuds, vanished into thin air. A classic agricultural theft, until the insurance company investigates. The payout demand isn't for $25,000. It's for $1.7 million — which would make this the single most valuable load of potatoes in human history. Because the driver, Donald Brown — D.B. — claimed they weren't just ordinary potatoes. He swore under oath they were 'Foundation Seed Stock', a secret, elite strain worth its weight in agricultural gold, destined for exclusive growers in Japan. The insurance firm had to decide: pay a fortune for the world's priciest tubers, or prove a potato was just a potato. They hired a private investigator who found nothing. No evidence of a high-value contract. No trace of the truck. The case went cold — officially unsolved, written off as a total loss. But forty years later, a retired claims adjuster would whisper the theory that still haunts Idaho's potato country: D.B. Tuber didn't just steal a truckload of potatoes. He stole an entire story — and might have sold the lie to a bigger thief, who cashed in on a phantom spud fortune that never existed.
