April 2, 1978. Two sets of footprints end at the icy edge of Lake Michigan. They belong to 23-year-old Hope College student Steven Kubacki. His snowshoes, backpack, and glasses are found stacked neatly on the shore near South Haven. He is not. The massive search turns up nothing. The case is presumed a tragic suicide. 15 months pass. Then, a phone call shatters a family's grief. It's Steven. Alive. He's in Pittsfield, Massachusetts. He claims he has no memory of his own disappearance. No memory of getting to Massachusetts. He says he just woke up one day—hundreds of miles away, over a year later—wearing different clothes, with a beard he didn't have before. He's carrying a backpack he doesn't recognize. The objects inside are strange, mundane. Authorities close the case. Steven is simply... back. He offers no explanation that fits a map. The footprints went nowhere. The memories are gone. And the unasked question hangs in the air of that Massachusetts room: who, or what, walked Steven Kubacki out of that lake?
