A 31-year-old MI6 officer is found dead, padlocked inside a sports bag, in his own bathtub. The keys are underneath him. Inside the bag. August 23, 2010. Gareth Williams hasn't answered his phone in over a week. Police force entry to his Pimlico flat. The front door was locked from the outside. On the bathroom floor sits a red North Face holdall, zipped shut and padlocked. Inside: Williams, folded into a fetal position. Dead for days. No cause of death is ever determined. No drugs. No poison. No trauma. Nothing. The bag is nearly impossible to enter from inside. Met Police experts try to lock themselves into an identical bag — and fail, repeatedly. His phone has been wiped remotely. The SIM card sits arranged in a deliberate geometric pattern on a table. Another person's DNA is traced on the padlock itself. £20,000 of women's designer clothing and wigs fill his wardrobe. His browser history visits bondage sites. The coroner rules: unlawful killing, likely criminally mediated. Scotland Yard overrides that conclusion. Probable accident. No charges. Case closed. But who wiped the phone? Whose DNA was on that lock? And why was the door locked from the outside?
