The black Mazda 626 sits in a parking lot behind an Asheville hospital, fourteen days after its owner vanished. Inside: a live puppy, a lipstick kiss smeared across the rear windshield, three water bottles, and a jacket that doesn't belong to the missing teenager. No body. No blood. Just questions. January 2, 2000. Zebb Quinn, eighteen, clocks out of his Wal-Mart shift at 9:00 PM. He follows a friend, Robert Owens, in a separate car — supposedly to test-drive a vehicle Zebb wants to buy. At 9:17 PM, surveillance at a convenience store captures both men inside. Zebb appears agitated on camera. Owens later tells police that Zebb received a page, saw something that panicked him, and peeled out — tailgating another car, tires screeching, gone. Zebb never comes home. Someone calls Wal-Mart the next morning claiming to be his aunt, saying he's sick and won't be in. Zebb has no aunt. Police trace the call to Robert Owens. Owens is charged with obstruction. He gives conflicting statements. The car turns up with that puppy, that kiss mark, those strange contents — and no forensic link to a killer. Then, fourteen years later, Owens is convicted of an unrelated triple homicide. Zebb Quinn has never been found. The puppy lived.
