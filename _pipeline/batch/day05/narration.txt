December 26th, 1900. The relief ship Hesperus finally reaches Eilean Mor lighthouse, twelve days late because of weather. No flag raised. No light lit. Joseph Moore climbs a hundred and sixty steps alone, calling names no one answers. Inside: a chair knocked on its back. A clock, stopped. A meal of mutton and potatoes sits untouched, days old. Three keepers gone — James Ducat, Thomas Marshall, Donald MacArthur. No bodies. No boat missing. Two of the three oilskin coats are still hanging — meaning whoever left last walked into the wind wearing nothing. Outside, the west landing is destroyed: an iron railing 110 feet above the sea, bent flat. A one-ton crane block torn clean off its mount. Here's the twist. The keepers' own logbook records a violent storm raging December 12th through 15th. But the mainland weather station, twenty miles east, logged calm seas that entire week. Three men wrote about a storm nobody else recorded. Then they vanished. No bodies ever surfaced. No sign they left by choice. The light at Eilean Mor still runs today — fully automated. Whatever came for them in 1900 was never identified, and never found.
