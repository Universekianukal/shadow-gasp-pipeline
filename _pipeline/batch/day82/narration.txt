March 24, 1998. 5:30 AM. The Rhapsody of the Seas sits docked in Curaçao harbor. Twenty-three-year-old Amy Lynn Bradley is asleep on the cabin balcony. Her father sees her there, closes the sliding door, goes back to bed. By 6:00 AM, Amy is gone. No sign of struggle. No blood. Her sandals and cigarettes left on the balcony table. Yellow-green bikini top, black shorts — that's all she took. The ship carries 2,000 passengers and 700 crew. Security cameras cover every corridor. Nobody saw her leave. FBI bloodhounds track Amy's scent to a crew-only stairwell, past the galley, to an area passengers cannot access. The trail dies there. A bartender named Alister Douglas reportedly spoke with Amy at the ship's disco hours before. He gave conflicting statements. No charges filed. Then the sightings begin. 1999 — a U.S. Navy sailor says a woman matching Amy approached him inside a Curaçao brothel. She said her name was Amy Bradley. She begged for help. Two men dragged her away before he could act. 2005 — anonymous photos arrive at the Bradley family home. A woman who looks exactly like Amy, posed in lingerie, eyes vacant. The FBI confirms a 'strong resemblance.' They cannot identify her. Amy Lynn Bradley has never been found.
