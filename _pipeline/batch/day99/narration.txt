April 1, 2006. 1:15 AM. Brian Shaffer, 27, second-year medical student at Ohio State, walks into the Ugly Tuna Saloona in Columbus. Surveillance cameras catch him stepping inside. He never comes back out. Three weeks earlier, his mother died of cancer. His father had died years before in a car accident. Brian was grieving. But that night he was out with friends for spring break. The bar had one main entrance. One set of cameras. Brian walked in. His friends stayed inside. At some point, he made a phone call. Then he vanished. When the bar closed, his friends assumed he'd gone home. His car was still parked outside. His phone pinged a tower miles away hours later. Then it went dead. Police reviewed the footage frame by frame. Brian enters. He never exits. The only other door was a service entrance wired to an alarm that never triggered. No one saw him leave. No one saw him stay. The lead detective worked the case for two years. Then, in 2008, he drove to a parking lot, called his wife, and took his own life. No note. No explanation. Brian Shaffer has been missing for 19 years. The footage still ends the same way — a man walking into a bar, and the door closing behind him.
