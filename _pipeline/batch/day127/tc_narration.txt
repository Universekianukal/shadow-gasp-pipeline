Constable Peter Godfrey signed off on his police patrol in Victoria, Australia, at 10:15 am. By 11:15, he was gone. His police motorcycle was discovered, still running, on the shoulder of the highway. His gloves, helmet, and one of his boots lay scattered nearby, as if simply discarded. The police revolver he always carried? Missing.

An unprecedented manhunt combed the region. The Royal Australian Air Force even flew over, searching for any sign. The official conclusion cited probable suicide. But no body was ever found near that lonely road. And the missing gun was a glaring contradiction — a man intending to take his own life would have used it.

Decades later, in 1954, a farmer’s shocking discovery: a skeletal human hand. It was found 60 miles away, near the town of Benalla. Investigators matched dental records. It was Godfrey’s. This final, grisly clue only deepened the mystery. How did he travel so far? And who, or what, placed his remains there? The official file is closed, but the questions remain, as cold and open as the Australian bush.
