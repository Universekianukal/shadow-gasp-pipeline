March 21, 1947. An anonymous call reports a smell coming from 2078 Fifth Avenue, Harlem. Police arrive. The front door won't budge — jammed solid by junk stacked from the inside. It takes two hours just to force a window. Inside: Homer Collyer, 65, blind, paralyzed, dead in a filthy bathrobe. Around him, newspapers stacked to the ceiling, broken furniture, fourteen pianos, the rusted chassis of a Model T. His brother Langley is nowhere in sight. A manhunt begins — sightings called in from three states. But he never left. It takes three weeks and 130 tons of debris hauled out by hand to find him. Ten feet from where Homer died. Crushed under one of his own booby traps, a rigged bale of newspapers meant to stop intruders. Here's the reversal: Langley had been dead for weeks — dead before Homer. Which means Homer, blind and unable to move, starved in total darkness, waiting for a brother who was already gone. No one knows the exact night Langley died. Or how many days Homer lay there, listening for footsteps that never came again.
