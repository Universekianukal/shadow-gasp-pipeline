June 12th, 1962. 7:15 a.m. A guard sweeps his flashlight across Cell 14-B and freezes. Frank Morris is asleep — except he isn't. It's a head. Soap, toilet paper, flesh-toned paint, and real human hair swept from the prison barbershop floor. Two cells over, the Anglin brothers' beds hold the same trick. All three inmates are gone. Behind each dummy head: a vent, widened over months with stolen spoons, hidden behind a cardboard grille painted to match the concrete. Beyond the vent, a utility corridor. Beyond that, the roof. On the shoreline, guards find scraps: a raft stitched from more than 50 stolen raincoats, sealed with rubber cement, paddles carved from plywood. San Francisco lies 1.25 miles away, across water that runs near 50 degrees, currents pulling at 9 miles an hour. In 1979, the FBI closes the case — not because they solved it. They just stop looking. Official ruling: presumed drowned. No bodies. No raft. No morgue photo. Nothing. Decades later, a letter arrives claiming to be from John Anglin, offering proof of survival in exchange for leniency. The FBI can't confirm it's real. Nobody can confirm any of it.
