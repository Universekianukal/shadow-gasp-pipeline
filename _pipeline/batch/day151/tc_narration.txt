December 31, 1980. A Brink's truck pulls up to Pan Am's cargo terminal at JFK airport. It's a holiday. The airport is quiet. The truck carries 100 barrels and five boxes. They're not full of coffee. Inside is platinum. 1,240 troy ounces. Valued at ninety million dollars today. The Brink's crew loads the cargo onto a pallet and moves it to a secure vault cage. They lock the cage. They seal it. They leave. When the crew returns after the holiday weekend, the warehouse is a ghost. The pallet is gone. The entire cage is gone. They call the FBI. No broken padlocks. No damaged doors. Just gone. The investigation begins. The feds find the cage in the parking lot. It's been cut open with a welding torch. The platinum is missing. The FBI suspects a single man: Joe 'the German' Koenig. The mastermind. They track his associates for years. They find one. He talks. The FBI plans a classic sting with an undercover agent. The break is coming. Then it vanishes. Joe 'the German' himself disappears. No money. No platinum. No convictions. The case grows cold. The platinum was melted down forty years ago. Recast into a fortune of jewelry and components, sold and scattered worldwide.
