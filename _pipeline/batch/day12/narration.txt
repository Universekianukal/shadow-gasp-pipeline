June 7, 1992, Springfield, Missouri. 2:15 a.m. Stacy McCall's car pulls into the driveway at 1717 East Delmar Street. She and her boyfriend just left three graduation parties. Inside: Sherrill Levitt, 47, her daughter Suzanne Streeter, 19, and now Stacy, 18. By morning, all three are gone.

No struggle. No blood. Their purses sit on the counter. Car keys still inside them. Both cars parked outside, untouched. The television is on. Sherrill's cigarette rests half-smoked in an ashtray. And outside, on the porch: the glass globe from the light fixture, shattered, lying in the grass — like someone reached up and broke it on their way out. Or in.

Police process the scene as an active abduction. No forced entry is ever found. No fingerprint matches anyone new. The reward climbs past $118,000 — one of the largest in Missouri history.

Then, decades in, a man on death row for another murder confesses to killing three women in Springfield that same week. Investigators fly out. He recants before they land.

Thirty-two years later, the house is gone. The women aren't. Nobody has ever been charged. Nobody knows what that porch light meant.
