April 19th, 1983. Mary Gillispie stops her school bus on a gravel road outside Circleville, Ohio, and walks toward a hand-painted sign nailed to a fence post. She's seen dozens like it — accusing her of an affair, taunting her by name. This one is wired to a loaded .25-caliber pistol, aimed at chest height. She grabs the wire. The gun fires. She survives by inches.

It started seven years earlier. 1976: an anonymous letter lands in her mailbox, detailing her daily route, naming a school superintendent, accusing her of adultery. More letters flood the school board. August 1977 — her husband Ron gets one that sends him into a rage. He drives off to confront someone, crashes into a tree on Five Points Road. Dead at the scene. A loaded gun sits on his passenger seat.

The letters don't stop. Over one hundred arrive by 1983. Police trace the booby-trap gun to Mary's brother-in-law, Paul Freshour. Handwriting experts convict him. Twenty-five years.

Then the letters keep coming — postmarked from Columbus, while Freshour sits locked in a cell with no outside contact. He's released in 1994. The letters, some say, never fully stopped.
