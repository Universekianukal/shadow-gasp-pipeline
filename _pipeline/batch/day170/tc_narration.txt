October 24, 1980. The SS Poet vanishes from radar screens. Carrying 34 crew and 6,000 tons of corn, it was bound for Egypt. Last contact: a routine check-in. Then, silence.

For days, the Coast Guard searches 100,000 square miles of Atlantic. No distress signal. No debris. Not a single life jacket.

The twist: weather reports show only moderate seas. No storms severe enough to sink a vessel this size. And the Poet had a reinforced hull, built for rough waters.

Theories flood in—piracy, structural failure, even sabotage. But without evidence, they're just ghosts. The ship's owners offer a $25,000 reward. Nothing surfaces.

Forty years later, the case remains cold. No wreckage. No answers. Just an empty stretch of ocean, holding its secret.
