5:09 AM. The roof of a cash depot in Västberga, Stockholm, blows open. A stolen Bell 206 Jet Ranger helicopter sits on top, rotors still turning. Seven masked men climb through the hole with explosives, ladders, and assault rifles. They knew exactly where to land. September 23, 2009 — and they had the depot's roof layout memorized. Inside, they shatter security glass and grab 39 million Swedish kronor — 4.6 million dollars — in under twenty minutes. Stockholm police scramble. But their response collapses before it starts. Every road around the depot is carpeted with caltrops — spiked metal stars that shred tires instantly. And at the police helicopter hangar in Myntkvartalet, officers find a package wired to look like a bomb. The entire air unit is grounded. No chase. No aerial pursuit. The robbers lift off and vanish into low clouds over the city. The helicopter turns up 160 kilometers away in a field near Arboga. Abandoned. Fueled, staged beside a getaway car. Four men were eventually convicted in 2017. But 38 of the 39 million was never recovered. And the person who planned it all — who knew the police protocols, the depot blind spots, the response times — was never identified.
