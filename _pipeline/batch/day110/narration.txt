107 people vanish into the Pacific without a single distress call. March 16, 1962. Flying Tiger Line Flight 739 — a Lockheed Super Constellation carrying 93 U.S. soldiers, bound for Saigon. At 2:45 AM, 280 miles west of Guam, the radio goes silent. No SOS. No wreckage on radar. Nothing. The U.S. military launches one of the largest search operations in Pacific history — 48 aircraft, 8 warships, scanning over 200,000 square miles of open ocean. Day after day. They find nothing. Not a seat cushion. Not a fuel slick. Not a body. Then a report surfaces. A Liberian tanker, 500 miles from the flight path. Its crew saw a bright orange flash in the sky at the exact time the plane would have been overhead. An explosion. The Air Force calls it the probable cause. Case closed — in-flight explosion, origin unknown. But here's what doesn't fit. The flash was seen 500 miles away — too far to confirm it was Flight 739. No debris was ever recovered to match the explosion theory. The Civil Aeronautics Board's final report, after 18 months, concludes: the cause is unknown. Sixty years later, not a single piece of Flight 739 has been found. 107 people disappeared from the sky. The ocean kept every secret.
