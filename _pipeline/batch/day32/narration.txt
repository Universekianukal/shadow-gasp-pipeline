4:03 a.m., July 6th, 2022. Elbert County, Georgia. A bomb rips through the Georgia Guidestones — a nineteen-foot granite monument standing since March 22nd, 1980. It weighed 237,746 pounds. Six slabs, inscribed in twelve languages, funded by a man who called himself R.C. Christian — a name nobody ever confirmed was real. For decades, conspiracy theorists called it the 'Satanic Ten Commandments,' a blueprint for a New World Order. QAnon forums obsessed over it. A sitting governor candidate had campaigned on tearing it down weeks before the blast. Security cameras caught a silver sedan fleeing the scene. No plate. No face. Then came the twist: hours after the explosion, before investigators even finished processing debris, the county demolished what remained — citing safety. The evidence, the monument, the mystery — gone in one afternoon. The GBI opened a case. Offered a reward. Nobody's ever been charged. And R.C. Christian's real identity? Still unknown, over forty years later. Two mysteries, stacked on top of each other, both unsolved — the bomber's face, and the founder's name.
