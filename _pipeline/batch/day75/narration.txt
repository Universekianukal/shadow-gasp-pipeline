July 14, 2007. A 14-year-old boy walks into Doncaster station and asks for a one-way ticket to London. The return costs fifty pence more. He says no. Andrew Gosden leaves home at 8:30 AM, skips school, empties his bank account — two hundred pounds. He leaves his phone charger plugged into the wall. His PlayStation sits untouched. He takes no spare clothes. CCTV catches him at King's Cross at 11:20 AM, walking out of the station alone. Then nothing. No phone call, no bank transaction, no sighting. He vanishes from one of the most surveilled cities on earth. For seventeen years the working theory was simple — a runaway. Posters went up. Searches dead-ended. The trail went cold. Then in 2024, police arrest two men. Charges: kidnap, facilitating sexual exploitation, human trafficking. The case shifts from missing person to potential homicide. But Andrew's body has never been found. The men are released on bail. No trial date is set. The file stays open. And that fifty-pence return ticket — the one he refused — remains the quiet detail no theory explains.
