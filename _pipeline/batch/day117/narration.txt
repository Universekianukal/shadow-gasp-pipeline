At 7:40 AM on October 1, 2015, the cargo ship El Faro sent a distress call from the center of Hurricane Joaquin. The 790-foot vessel was listing 15 degrees, taking on water, and had lost propulsion. Thirty-three people were aboard. Zero survived. The ship was sailing from Jacksonville, Florida to San Juan, Puerto Rico — a route Captain Michael Davidson had run dozens of times. He told the crew he would skirt the hurricane's edge by 90 nautical miles. He was consulting a weather forecast 12 hours old. Joaquin had shifted south and intensified to a Category 4. When the engines died in 30-foot swells, the ship drifted straight into the storm's strongest quadrant. Water flooded through an open scuttle — a hatch that should have been sealed. The ship's Voyage Data Recorder captured 26 hours of audio: alarms, confusion, and a captain who believed until the end they could ride it out. Seven months later, the Navy located the wreck 15,000 feet below the surface. The VDR was recovered intact — one of the deepest black-box retrievals ever attempted. But the recording cuts out 15 minutes before the sinking. Whether El Faro rolled, capsized, or plunged bow-first — no one survived to say.
