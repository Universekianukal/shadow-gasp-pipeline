August 28th, 2003. Erie, Pennsylvania. A man kneels in a parking lot, hands cuffed, a heavy metal collar bolted around his neck. Wires. A ticking clock. His name is Brian Wells — a pizza delivery driver. Forty minutes earlier, he'd robbed a bank with a cane hiding a loaded shotgun. He walked out with $8,702 — a fraction of the $250,000 demanded in a note taped to his collar. That note ordered a scavenger hunt across town, each clue timed to the minute. Police surround him now. The bomb squad is still nine minutes out. At 3:18 PM, live on a news camera, the device detonates. Brian Wells dies in the street, surrounded by officers who couldn't save him in time. For years, investigators call him a hostage. Then the twist: the FBI says Wells may have helped plan his own execution — recruited into the plot, then betrayed by the people who built the bomb strapped to his neck. Marjorie Diehl-Armstrong, the mastermind, is convicted in 2010. She dies in prison in 2017, still insisting there's more to the story. Nobody has ever fully explained the scavenger hunt notes. Some clues were never solved.
