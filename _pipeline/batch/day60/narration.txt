December 6, 1928. A search plane banking over the Colorado River spots a wooden scow drifting alone through the Grand Canyon — upright, fully loaded, no one aboard. Bessie Hyde's lipstick sits on the seat. The camera is untouched. Six weeks earlier, she and her new husband Glen launched this same boat to set the speed record through the Canyon. Bessie would be the first woman ever to do it. They carried 40 days of provisions and a marriage license dated October 1928. On November 18, photographer Emery Kolb took their last known photo at his riverside studio. They were smiling. Then — 26 days of silence. The scow was found near Mile 232. No bodies. No struggle. No footprints on shore. 43 years later, legendary river runner Georgie Clark announced at a dinner party that she was Bessie Hyde — that she'd stabbed Glen and walked out of the canyon alive. Books were written. A documentary was filmed. Then in 2010, DNA testing on Clark's salvaged hair proved she was never Bessie at all. The Colorado River took the Hydes somewhere between Mile 145 and Mile 232. 97 years later, it has never given them back.
