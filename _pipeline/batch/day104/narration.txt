July 11, 1996. A man is found nearly naked, face-up in a Knoxville parking lot. Thousands of dollars in cash scattered around his body. Two passports. No wallet. He is dead. His name was Blair Adams. A construction foreman from Surrey, British Columbia. Thirty-one years old. Two weeks earlier, he told his mother someone was trying to kill him. He quit his job. Withdrew over $6,000 in cash from multiple bank accounts. He tried to cross into the United States. Border agents turned him back. Twice. Third attempt, he made it through. He drove 2,500 miles across America, buying gas, changing vehicles, burning through cash. He reached Knoxville on July 10. Checked into a motel. Left within minutes. A gas station attendant saw him that night — pacing, disheveled, unable to stand still. His body was found at 7:30 the next morning behind a construction supply company. His clothes had been ripped off. He had $4,000 still on him. The autopsy listed cause of death as a heart attack. But the coroner found bruises across his abdomen. Scratches on his chest. Marks consistent with someone grabbing him. Holding him down. No suspect was ever identified. No one was ever charged. The cash was never taken. Whatever Blair Adams was running from — it may have found him first.
