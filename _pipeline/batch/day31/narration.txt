Riverside General Hospital, February 19th, 1994. 8:15 PM. Paramedics wheel in Gloria Ramirez, 31, dying of cervical cancer. Minutes later, nurse Susan Kane draws blood — and stops. The syringe smells like garlic. Manila-colored particles float inside it. She hands it off. The resident faints. Another nurse smells ammonia and drops. Within ten minutes, five ER staff are down, gasping, seizing. The hospital evacuates 23 people from the emergency room. Gloria dies at 8:50 PM — 35 minutes after arrival. Investigators expect poison. Toxicology finds nothing. Livermore National Lab spends months testing a theory: Gloria used DMSO cream for pain, and inside her body it somehow turned into a lethal gas. Chemists call the math impossible — the reaction shouldn't happen in living tissue. No official cause is ever confirmed. Five hospital workers stay sick for months — nerve damage, breathing problems. The county calls it mass hysteria. The family calls it a cover-up. They fight for years over what her body actually did in that room — and lose the chance for answers when her remains are cremated before a second autopsy. The gas, if it existed, left no trace. It just left five careers ruined.
