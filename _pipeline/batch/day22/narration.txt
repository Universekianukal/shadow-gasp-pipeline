A collie clears the granite parapet in one motion. No hesitation. Fifty feet down, rocks and the Overtoun Burn. The owner is still holding the leash. Since the 1960s, more than fifty dogs have jumped from this exact fifteen-foot stretch of Overtoun Bridge near Dumbarton, Scotland. Not near it. From it. Built in 1895 for Lord Overtoun, the bridge sits half a mile from his old mansion. Witnesses notice a pattern: it's almost always dry, almost always sunny, and the dogs always leap from the same right-hand parapet. In 2014, SSPCA officer David Sexton floated an answer: mink scent trapped under the bridge, invisible to dogs whose eyeline stops exactly at the wall's height. A tidy theory. Except it doesn't explain 1994, when a local man carried his baby onto that same bridge, convinced the child was the Antichrist, and threw him over — then tried to jump himself. The baby survived. Locals still call the spot a 'thin place,' where the veil to the other side wears down. No barrier has ever been built. And some dogs that survive the fall, months later, come back and jump again.
