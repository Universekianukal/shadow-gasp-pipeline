Nine hikers vanish in the Ural Mountains, and their tent is slashed open — from the inside. February 1, 1959. Kholat Syakhl — 'Dead Mountain.' Nine experienced skiers, led by Igor Dyatlov, camp for the night. Something drives them out. Barefoot. In minus twenty-five degrees Celsius. Search parties find the tent three weeks later, cut open, boots and coats still inside. Footprints — some just socks — lead 500 meters downhill toward a tree line. Two bodies turn up under a cedar, remains of a small fire nearby. Three more are scattered between there and the tent, as if trying to climb back. It takes until May to find the last four, buried under four meters of snow in a ravine. Their injuries don't fit a bear, or a fall. Crushed ribs, a fractured skull — but no broken skin. One woman is missing her tongue and eyes. Some clothing tests positive for radiation. Soviet investigators close the case citing 'a compelling unknown force.' Files stay sealed for decades. In 2019, Russia reopens it. Official verdict: avalanche. It still doesn't explain the tongue. Or the eyes. Or why nobody agrees.
