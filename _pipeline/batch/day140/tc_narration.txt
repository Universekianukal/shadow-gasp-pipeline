Three miles off Florida's coast, the 25-foot cabin cruiser 'Sea Bird' drifts silently toward shore. Its engine is running, a breakfast is set on the table. But no one is on board. On July 19, 1978, John H. Cobb and his family of four vanished. A thorough Coast Guard search turns up nothing. No distress call, no debris. The pattern suggests the Cobbs stepped off the boat for a moment, but never returned. A decade later, a chilling detail surfaces: in 1988, a fisherman snags a single tennis shoe off the very same coast. Inside is a fragment of human bone. DNA testing in the 2000s confirms it belongs to the Cobb family's teenage son. It's the only physical trace ever found. But the discovery only deepens the mystery. The shoe and bone showed no evidence of marine predator damage. The location was too far north for the boat's last known position. The case closes not with a solution, but with a final, impossible question: how does an entire family disappear from a moving boat without a single sign of struggle, only for one fragment to return 10 years and 200 miles off course?
