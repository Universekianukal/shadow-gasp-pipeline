October 16, 1984. A phone rings in a farmhouse in Lépanges, eastern France. Christine Villemin picks up. A man's voice says: "I have taken the boy. I have taken revenge." The line goes dead. Her four-year-old son, Grégory, has vanished from the front yard. His body surfaces that evening in the Vologne River — hands and feet bound, drowned, fully clothed. But this was not random. For three years prior, the Villemin family had received anonymous letters and calls from someone who signed themselves "The Crow." Over 30 letters. Obsessive. Threatening. Full of private grievances only a relative would know. Investigators focused on Bernard Laroche, a distant cousin. He was arrested, confessed to a colleague, then retracted everything. Released for lack of evidence. Then on March 29, 1985, Grégory's father Jean-Marie tracked Laroche down and shot him dead in the street. He served four years for murder. The reversal: DNA testing in 2004 could not link Laroche to the crime. The case closed in 2008, reopened in 2017. New genetic analysis on the letters — inconclusive. The Crow was never identified. Grégory's killer remains unnamed. The last known letter arrived in 2004. Nobody knows if another will come.
