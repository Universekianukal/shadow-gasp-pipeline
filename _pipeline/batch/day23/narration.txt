Marianna, Florida. Boys as young as five were sent here — for truancy, for smoking, for being 'incorrigible.' They called the punishment building 'The White House.' Inside, boys were strapped down and beaten with a three-foot leather strap the guards nicknamed 'Black Beauty.' Some couldn't sit for weeks. Some never walked out at all. Officially, the state recorded 31 deaths since 1914. In 2012, forensic anthropologist Erin Kimmerle brought ground-penetrating radar to the campus cemetery. She found 55 graves. Almost double the official count. Some were children. Some had never been named at all. The school didn't close because of this. It shut down in 2011 — a full year before the digging even began — over a budget shortfall, a hundred and eleven years after it opened. No one was ever charged. No one went to prison. Right now, dozens of those bodies remain unidentified. DNA samples sit in a lab, waiting for a match. Somewhere, families are still checking every update, still hoping the next name released is theirs.
