A man's body lies on Rosses Point beach, Sligo, Ireland. June 18, 2009. Fully clothed. No wallet, no phone, no ID. €130 in cash. Three days earlier, he arrived by bus carrying a single purple bag and a name that doesn't exist. He checked into the Sligo City Hotel as Peter Bergmann. Address: Ainstetersgasse 14, Vienna, Austria. The street doesn't exist. The name doesn't exist. Over 72 hours, hotel CCTV captures the same ritual. He walks out carrying the bag. Returns empty-handed. Security cameras across Sligo record him dropping documents into public bins — anything traceable. He cuts every label from every garment he owns. Then on June 18, he checks out at 10:45 AM and walks three kilometers to the beach. Cause of death: drowning. No drugs, no alcohol, no signs of foul play. Here is where the case breaks. There was no killer. He erased himself — methodically, deliberately, over three days. Fingerprints: no match in any database. Dental records: nothing. Interpol: nothing. No missing person report on earth fits. Fourteen years later, no one has claimed him. No one has ever reported him missing.
