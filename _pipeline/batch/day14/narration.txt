6:20 AM, December 11th, 1978. Six masked men storm the Lufthansa cargo terminal at JFK. Sixty-four minutes later, they vanish with $5 million in cash and $875,000 in jewelry — the largest cash robbery in American history at the time. No alarms triggered. No shots fired. The tip came from a JFK cargo worker named Louis Werner, buried in gambling debt. The mastermind: Jimmy Burke, a Lucchese associate who trusted no one. The getaway van was found four days later in a Brooklyn parking lot. It was supposed to be torched. It wasn't. One fingerprint survived — enough to convict the man who forgot to burn it. Then the real horror started. Within a year, at least eight people tied to the heist were dead. Shot in meat trucks. Frozen in freezer trucks. Found in car trunks. Burke, investigators believed, was erasing his own crew to keep a bigger share. He was never charged for the robbery itself. Only Werner went to prison for it. Of the $5.875 million stolen, only about $20,000 was ever recovered. To this day, nobody knows where the rest went — or how many bodies it actually cost.
