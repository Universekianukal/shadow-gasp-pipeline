February 24, 1971. A single-engine Cessna 182 vanishes from radar over the snowy ridges of Mount Rainier. Pilot Martin McNally, his wife, and a close-knit hunting party are gone. The search, led by the Civil Air Patrol, drags on for two days. No debris. No signal. Then, a call to the FBI office in Seattle. A male voice, calm and precise, demands a ransom for the seven missing passengers. He knows their names and their itinerary. The demand: $500,000 in gold bullion. He swears they are alive, held captive. The FBI scrambles. Ransom drops are arranged, but the caller never appears. A phantom. The case officially closed in 1973: cause unknown. The plane and its occupants have never been found. But the ransom call logged in the FBI file remains. A perfect, unsolved fraud, or a genuine signal from the dead? The caller's identity, like the wreckage, is buried beneath the snow.
