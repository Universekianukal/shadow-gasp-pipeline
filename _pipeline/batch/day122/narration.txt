Gertrude Baniszewski, a struggling 37-year-old mother, agrees to watch 16-year-old Sylvia Likens for $20 a week. Sylvia’s parents are carnival workers, always on the road. Within weeks, Gertrude’s house becomes a prison. The $20 stops arriving. Sylvia is forced to eat her own vomit, burned with cigarettes, and branded with a hot needle that reads, ‘I’m a prostitute and proud of it.’ In the basement, neighborhood children, led by Gertrude’s own kids, are invited to join the torture. For three months, no one intervenes. The eventual trial reveals over 150 bruises, cuts, and burns. 16 witnesses. The reversal? In a final act of cruelty, Gertrude forces Sylvia’s little sister, Jenny, to participate. The court sentences Gertrude to life—but parole hearings begin in less than twelve years. She serves only twenty. The house at 3850 East New York Street was demolished, but the neighbors—dozens who heard the screams—never called the police.
