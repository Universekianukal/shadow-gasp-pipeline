Six minutes before he vanishes forever, Frederick Valentich radios air traffic control with four words: 'It's not an aircraft.' October 21st, 1978. He's 20 years old, flying a rented Cessna 182 across Bass Strait — fifty miles of open water between mainland Australia and King Island. At 7:06 PM he reports a large unknown aircraft passing 1,000 feet above him. It isn't on radar. He describes a shiny metallic surface and a green light, moving at speeds his Cessna can't match. He says it's playing games with him. Then his engine starts running rough. At 7:12 PM, his last full transmission: 'That strange aircraft is hovering on top of me again.' Then a burst of metallic, scraping static. Then silence. Air traffic control scrambles a search across 1,000 square miles of ocean. No wreckage surfaces. No oil slick. No mayday call. No body. Investigators quietly point to disorientation, maybe suicide — but flight logs show no distress before that broadcast. Four years later, a light aircraft engine washes ashore near the search zone. It's never confirmed to be his. The file stays open in Canberra to this day. Bass Strait has never given up the plane.
