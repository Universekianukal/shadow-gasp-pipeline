November 25, 1809. A British diplomat steps behind his carriage outside an inn in Perleberg, Prussia — and never reappears. Benjamin Bathurst had spent weeks in Vienna, secretly negotiating Austria into war against Napoleon. He was traveling under a false name, carrying 4,000 Austrian ducats in gold. His valet sat inside the carriage. Bathurst walked to the front to inspect the horses. Ten seconds. That's all it took. When the carriage pulled forward, he was gone. The road was empty. The fields were flat and open. No sound. No struggle. No body. Weeks later, searchers found his pantaloons in a ravine two miles from the inn — slashed open and emptied. A local butcher was seen wearing Bathurst's distinctive fur coat. He claimed he'd bought it from two strangers. A man named Schmidt was arrested. Then released. Here's the twist. The famous 'vanished into thin air' story — the one that filled books for two centuries — was invented entirely by Bathurst's sister, who never visited Perleberg. She needed the disappearance to be supernatural. The real question was always simpler and darker. What did the valet know? He gave three different statements to police. Then he walked out of Perleberg. And was never seen again either.
