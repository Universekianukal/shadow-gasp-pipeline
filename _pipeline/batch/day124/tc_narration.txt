December 1913. Author Ambrose Bierce, age 71, mails his last letter from Washington, D.C. He informs his secretary he is heading into Mexico to "disappear from the face of the earth." He is not joking. Bierce, famous for his cynical short stories and his Civil War service, joins Pancho Villa's rebel army in Chihuahua. He sends a few dispatches, then… silence. The U.S. consulate starts looking in 1914. They find a lead: a letter from a missionary reporting Bierce witnessed the execution of a federal officer in Icamole, and was last seen heading toward the Sierra Madre. A search party finds nothing. Decades later, two conflicting stories surface. A former rebel soldier, Jose Vasquez, swears Bierce was executed by a firing squad for intervening in a dispute between Villa and his lieutenant. His body, Vasquez says, was buried in a mass grave. Yet in 1938, a Texas journalist named Ed Doherty claims he tracked down a witness in Sinaloa who saw an old gringo, alone, walk into the desert without water or supplies. Two endings, one mystery. The author, who wrote so often of sudden, violent death and vanishing, gave himself the perfect final act. The investigation is closed. The question remains wide open.
