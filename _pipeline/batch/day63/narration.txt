The phone rings at 4:00 AM. A producer at KIMT-TV calls her morning anchor, Jodi Huisentruit. Jodi's late for the 6:00 broadcast. She answers, says she's on her way. She never makes it. June 27, 1995. Mason City, Iowa. Jodi is 27. Her apartment parking lot tells the whole story. Her red Mazda Miata sits unlocked, keys still hanging from the driver's door. One shoe on the ground. Hair spray, scissors, her microphone — scattered across the asphalt in a trail. Drag marks in the dirt lead away from the car. A heel print pressed into the ground beside the door. Police arrive within the hour. And find nothing. No body. No blood. No witnesses. The lot sits 200 feet from the nearest apartment. A violent struggle happened here at 4 AM, and nobody heard a sound. Within days, over 1,000 tips pour in from 48 states. A white van was seen near her building between 3:30 and 4:15 AM. The driver was never identified. Three decades pass. Jodi has never been found. No one has ever been charged. The parking lot was repaved years ago — the drag marks, the heel print, the last physical evidence, all buried under fresh asphalt. Someone in Mason City knows what happened at 4 AM on a Tuesday morning. They've stayed silent for 29 years.
