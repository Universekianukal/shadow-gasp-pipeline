On March 27, 1934, Baroness Eloise von Wagner Bosquet vanished from Floreana Island — a speck of volcanic rock in the Galápagos, 600 miles off Ecuador. No radio. No airstrip. No witnesses. She arrived in 1932 with two lovers, a rifle collection, and a plan to build a luxury hotel for passing yachtsmen. Three settlers already shared the island: a German dentist named Friedrich Ritter, his lover Dore Strauch, and the Wittmer family — Heinz, Margaret, and their newborn son. Tensions erupted over water, mail, and a single hand-carved mailbox post. The Baroness threatened neighbors at gunpoint. Ritter published articles calling her insane. Then on March 27, she and lover Robert Phillipson disappeared. The remaining lover, Rudolf Lorenz, told the Wittmers the couple had sailed to Tahiti on a passing yacht. No such yacht ever arrived in Tahiti. Three weeks later, Lorenz's decomposed body washed up on Marchena Island — 50 miles north, mummified by sun and salt. Cause of death: undetermined. That November, Dr. Ritter died of food poisoning. Dore signed conflicting statements — once hinting she'd poisoned him herself. No bodies. No yacht. Three deaths on one island in eight months. Margaret Wittmer lived to 95, repeating one version of events until her final breath — contradicting every other account.
