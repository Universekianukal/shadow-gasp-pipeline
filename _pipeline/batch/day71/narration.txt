1983. A Philadelphia street worker spots something embedded in the asphalt at South 7th and Spruce. A tile. Pressed flush into the road surface. The message: TOYNBEE IDEA — IN MOVIE 2001 — RESURRECT DEAD — ON PLANET JUPITER. No signature. No explanation. Four lines of cryptic text baked into linoleum, sealed with asphalt crack filler, placed to be driven over for decades. By 1990, over 130 tiles appear across Philadelphia. Then New York. Then Washington DC. Then Santiago, Chile. All identical in style. All referencing historian Arnold Toynbee, Stanley Kubrick's film 2001, and resurrecting the dead on Jupiter. For 25 years, nobody knows who's planting them. Then investigators trace a residential address — 1417 South 7th Street — where dozens of the earliest tiles cluster in a tight radius. The homeowner is Severino Verna, a tile setter by trade. He died in 2003. Case closed? Not quite. New tiles keep appearing. In 2007. In 2011. In 2019. Some match the original materials and craftsmanship perfectly. Others are crude imitations. Someone — or several someones — is still out there, still laying messages into asphalt, still waiting for Jupiter.
