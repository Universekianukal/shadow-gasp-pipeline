February 24th, 1978. Five men leave a Chico State basketball game and drive into a blizzard-white nothing. Jack Madruga's Mercury Montego is found the next morning, 68 miles off the route home, nose-down in a snowbank near Rackerby — keys in the ignition, doors unlocked, half a tank of gas. It isn't even stuck that badly. They could have driven out. For three months, nothing. Then on June 4th, hunters find Ted Weiher inside an abandoned hunting camp, 19 miles from the car. He's lost over 100 pounds. His shoes are on the wrong feet. Socks stuffed on his hands like gloves. His watch stopped weeks earlier. Here's the reversal: the cabin had a working wood stove, dry firewood, and matches sitting right there. He never lit it. He froze to death surrounded by everything he needed to live. Two more bodies turn up scattered in a nearby ravine. A fourth is found later that year. The fifth man, Gary Mathias, is never found. Not a bone, not a shoe, not in 46 years. Nobody has ever explained why five men with nowhere to be drove seventy miles into the mountains and never turned around.
