A priest in black vestments prays before an empty glass case in his rectory. It's November 2009. The Church of St. Matthew the Apostle is missing its most precious artifact: a 500-year-old ivory cross, intricately carved. And Father Ricardo F. de Leon, the 57-year-old priest who cared for it, is gone. The cross itself is valued at 600,000 Philippine pesos. But for the devout, it's priceless. Father Rico, as they called him, was last seen on November 16th. His maroon Mitsubishi Lancer sedan was found abandoned near the San Juanico Bridge—a 2-kilometer structure connecting islands. His wallet, phone, and glasses were left in the car. Family receives a text demanding 3 million pesos. Yet the cross is never mentioned. Police suspect a kidnapping. The pattern breaks. No ransom call ever comes. The case closes with a court declaring the priest dead in absentia. But the missing cross remains a silent, sacred gap. Did a thief pose as a kidnapper? Did faith and greed collide in a single, unsolved vanishing? Today, the church displays a replica. The real cross, and the man who kept it, are part of a mystery where the only clue is what was taken, and who was meant to follow.
