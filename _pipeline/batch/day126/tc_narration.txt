Pac West Airlines Flight 799. March 6th, 1971. A Boeing 727 is mid-flight over Eastern Washington when a man hands a note to the lone flight attendant. It claimed a bomb was in his briefcase. He wanted half a million dollars. The hijacker, soft-spoken, instructed the pilot to land at Spokane Field. The ransom was delivered in sacks: $500,000 in $100 bills, wrapped in brown paper. He was meticulous. He allowed every passenger off the plane first. He even let the stewardess go. As ground crews gassed up the jet, he watched the terminal… and then vanished. The plane was left behind, engines running. Only the hijacker’s note and his empty briefcase remained. An FBI hunt tore apart the terminal, traced the serial numbers on every bill. Nothing. The money, a half-ton of cash, had evaporated. It would be eight years before a single bill from the haul surfaced. In Las Vegas. But the trail went cold again. The hijacker’s identity remained a cipher – a polite ghost who executed the perfect score and then disappeared into the myth of the Pacific Northwest.
