A woman in a white nurse's uniform walks into a maternity ward at Michael Reese Hospital, Chicago, April 26, 1964. She picks up a two-day-old boy named Paul Fronczak. She walks out the front door. No one stops her. She is never seen again. Chicago police pull 250,000 interviews. The FBI files 9,000 reports. Nothing. For two years, the Fronczak family searches. Then in 1966, a toddler turns up abandoned outside a store in Newark, New Jersey. FBI agents compare earprints — the accepted forensic method of the era — and declare this child is Paul. The Fronczaks take him home. The case closes. He grows up as Paul Fronczak in a Chicago suburb. Decades pass. In 2012, he takes a commercial DNA test. Zero match to the Fronczak family. The child raised as Paul was never Paul. He was someone else's stolen or lost son — and no one knew for 46 years. The real Paul Fronczak, the two-day-old taken from that maternity ward, has never been found. No body. No record. No trail. The woman in the nurse's uniform vanished so completely that six decades later, her name, her face, and what she did with the baby remain completely unknown.
