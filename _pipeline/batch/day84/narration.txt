On April 15, 2005, a district attorney vanishes. Not a rookie — Ray Gricar, the top prosecutor of Centre County, Pennsylvania. Thirty years in law enforcement. He tells his girlfriend he's taking a scenic drive along the Susquehanna River. She never hears from him again. His red Mini Cooper sits in a parking lot in Lewisburg, 50 miles from home. Keys gone. Phone off. No blood, no struggle, no witnesses. Then the river gives up pieces. July — his government-issued laptop, pulled from the Susquehanna. September — the hard drive, found separately, 100 yards downstream. Both too damaged to recover a single file. But investigators find something on his home computer. Days before he vanished, someone searched how to destroy a hard drive. How to fry a hard drive. Intentional. Deliberate. Then the shadow everyone whispers about. In 1998, Gricar declined to prosecute Jerry Sandusky after a mother reported her son had been assaulted. That case file later disappeared from his office. Years before Sandusky's arrest shattered Penn State. One more detail. Gricar's own brother drowned in 1996. Ruled accidental. Two men. Same family. Both gone near water. No body. No note. No confession. The DA who held the county's darkest secrets was never found.
