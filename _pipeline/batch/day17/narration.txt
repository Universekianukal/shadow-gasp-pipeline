January 17th, 1950. Boston. Seven masked men walk into the Brink's building at 165 Prince Street wearing chauffeur's coats and rubber Halloween masks. In fourteen minutes, they empty the vault. $1,218,211 in cash. $1,557,183 in checks and money orders. Total haul: $2,775,395. No alarms triggered. No shots fired. They vanish into the Boston night. The FBI floods the case with 5,000 leads. Nothing sticks. Six years pass. The statute of limitations is about to expire — days away. Then the case doesn't break because of a fingerprint or a wiretap. It breaks because of money. One gang member, Joseph 'Specs' O'Keefe, feels shorted on his cut. His own crew has him shot to keep him quiet. He survives — and starts talking to the FBI, eleven days before the statute runs out. Eight men are convicted in 1956. But of the $2,775,395 stolen that night, only $58,000 is ever recovered. Two point seven million dollars. Gone. No vault, no account, no grave has ever given it up. Where did the rest go? Nobody who knew ever said.
