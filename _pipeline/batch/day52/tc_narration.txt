January 4, 2012. A single image appears on 4chan. Black background. White text. "We are looking for highly intelligent individuals." No signature. No context. Within hours, someone finds something buried inside the image itself — a hidden message pulled out using steganography software. It points to a cipher. The cipher leads to a website. The site vanishes in sixty seconds if you're too slow. Solvers spend weeks decoding Mayan numerals, Arthurian legend, a 1518 cipher, and a hidden occult text. Physical posters turn up in fourteen cities — Warsaw, Paris, Seattle, Seoul — each with a QR code and a phone number reciting more numbers in a robotic voice. Then silence. Whoever solves the final puzzle is contacted privately. Recruited, some say, into something. Nobody who wins ever speaks publicly again. Three more puzzles follow through 2014. Then Cicada goes dark. Permanently. No group has ever claimed responsibility. No government has confirmed involvement. A handful of winners are rumored to exist. Not one has ever come forward.
