20,000 feet over the Bavarian Alps, a man in a Lufthansa cockpit pulls a gun on his own crew. It's February 27, 1978. Flight 182 from Frankfurt to Munich. He announces his name: 'Captain Jürgen Schumann' — but he’s not the real captain. He's a hijacker. His demand: 15 million deutsche marks and the release of two Red Army Faction prisoners. The real Captain Schumann is on the floor behind him. They land in Munich. Authorities agree. A police van delivers the cash, in 500-mark bills. The hijacker has a new instruction: take off again. He orders the crew to fly low over a pre-marked forest clearing outside Augsburg. A canvas bag, stuffed with millions, is tossed from the rear emergency exit. It tumbles into the pines. The plane lands in East Berlin. The hijacker disappears into East German custody — a known RAF sympathizer, protected by the Stasi. West German police scramble to the drop zone. They find nothing. Not a single bill. The 15 million marks — roughly $8.5 million then, over $40 million today — is simply gone. One witness, a forester, reported seeing a white van speeding away. No license plate. No suspects. No arrests. The ransom vanished into the fog of the Cold War. The case was closed, not solved.
