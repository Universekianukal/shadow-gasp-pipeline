A four-year-old girl hides beneath her dead mother's body in the dark. She does not make a sound for eight hours. Forty feet away, her seven-year-old sister lies beaten and shot on a forest road near Lake Annecy, France. September 5, 2012. Four people are dead — Saad al-Hilli, a 50-year-old Iraqi-born British aerospace engineer, his wife Iqbal, her 74-year-old mother, and a passing French cyclist named Sylvain Mollier, 45, who rode into the wrong clearing at the wrong time. Twenty-five bullets from an antique Luger P06 handgun. The killer vanished into the Alps. No DNA. No fingerprints. No witnesses — except a motorcyclist in a dark helmet, seen speeding away, never identified. The al-Hillis' BMW was found locked from the inside, engine still running. Saad's brother Zaid was arrested in 2013 over an inheritance dispute — then released without charge. Saad's work at Surrey Satellite Technology raised defense-contract questions. His family's ties to Saddam Hussein's regime surfaced and stalled. Twelve years later, the case is still open. The Luger has never been recovered. And somewhere, that four-year-old — now a teenager — has never fully spoken about what she saw that afternoon.
