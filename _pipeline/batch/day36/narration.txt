April 18, 2007. A fishing boat spots a yacht drifting off Cape Upstart, on Australia's Great Barrier Reef. No one answers the radio. Rescuers board the Kaz II and find the engine still running. Sails up, but flapping loose. A meal sits on the table, untouched. A laptop glows, still powered on. Three life jackets sit folded, still dry. Three men are missing: Derek Batten, 56, and brothers Peter and James Tunstead, 69 and 63. No distress call was ever sent. No blood. No struggle. No storm in the forecast that week. Search crews scour over 15,000 square miles of open water. They find nothing. Not a body, not a shoe, not a wallet. The coroner's final theory, delivered in 2008, sounds almost too strange to believe: one man's hat blew off. He leaned over the swim platform to grab it. He fell. The others reached to pull him back — and went in too. The boat, engine still engaged, kept drifting forward without them. Three men treading water, watching their own yacht sail on without them. To this day, not one body has ever washed ashore.
