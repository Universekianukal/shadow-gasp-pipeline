Two men walk into the National Gallery at 6 AM. It’s February 23rd, 1986. A single guard is on duty. They hand him a note. ‘We have a gun.’ The heist takes 50 seconds. They steal only one painting: Edvard Munch’s *The Scream*. The frame is left leaning against the wall, its wire cut. Norway closes its borders. Police search every port. Nothing. A ransom demand arrives: one million kroner. But the thieves go silent. For three months, there is no word. The case breaks on May 7th. An undercover detective, posing as a Getty rep, arranges a hotel meeting. The painting is returned unharmed. Four men are arrested. Their motive? A diversion. The theft was meant to cover a document-forgery ring. They never meant to damage the art. Yet conservators find a small, damp stain in the painting’s corner. Damage from its hiding place. All four men confessed. All were convicted. But not one ever revealed where *The Scream* was hidden for those three long months. The exact room, the box, the closet—that secret died with them.
