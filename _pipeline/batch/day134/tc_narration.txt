On Tuesday, October 3, 1978, fifty-two-year-old Judith Smith takes a bus from her home in Dayton, Ohio. Her destination is a Greyhound station 130 miles away in Cincinnati. She's meeting her husband, Robert, for a weekend trip. He's waiting. He will wait for four hours. Judy never gets off the bus.

Driver statements are clear: she was on the 2:15 p.m. express. The Cincinnati terminal is a simple, open building. There are only two exits. Surveillance footage is reviewed. Judy does not appear. Police conclude she vanished between the bus door and the terminal door—a distance of 40 feet. In broad daylight. With witnesses. Her purse, containing $127, is found on a seat.

The case file grows thick with dead ends. Then, a twist: a psychic's vision leads searchers 700 miles north, to a remote Michigan wood. They find a woman's skeleton. Dental records confirm it is not Judy Smith. The psychic's false lead consumes hundreds of man-hours, derailing the official investigation for months.

Judy Smith remains one of the most baffling missing persons cases in Ohio history. No evidence of foul play. No credible sightings. Her Social Security number has never been used. She simply evaporated in a 40-foot gap, leaving behind a single question that echoes in that empty space between the bus and the door: how?
