April 18th, 1943. Four boys poach for bird's eggs in Hagley Wood, England. One of them, Bob Farmer, reaches into a hollow wych elm tree — and pulls out a human skull. Beneath it: a nearly complete skeleton stuffed inside the trunk. Taffeta fabric jammed in the mouth. One hand missing entirely, severed and buried thirteen feet away, like a trophy. The boys say nothing for days — they'd been poaching, and confessing meant admitting the crime. When police finally arrive, pathologist James Webster estimates she'd been dead at least eighteen months, since around October 1941. She's roughly thirty-five, four foot ten, with crooked lower teeth and a gold ring nearby. Dental records turn up nothing. No missing person matches. The case goes cold within a year. Then, in late 1944, graffiti starts appearing on walls across Birmingham. Chalked in a shaky hand: 'Who put Bella down the Wych Elm?' Police never trace the writer. Some think it's a witness. Some think it's the killer, taunting them. The graffiti keeps reappearing on the same stone obelisk for over fifty years. Last spotted in 1999. Bella still has no name.
