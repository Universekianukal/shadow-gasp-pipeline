March 4th, 1918. The USS Cyclops leaves Barbados carrying 306 men and 10,800 tons of manganese ore — and never arrives. No SOS. No wreckage. No lifeboats. Nothing. The ship left Rio de Janeiro on February 16th already dangerously overloaded, riding low in the water. At Barbados, instead of unloading, the captain took on more ore. Captain George Worley was strange even by Navy standards — German-born, prone to pacing the deck in a bowler hat and long underwear, once accused by his own crew of being a spy. Nine days later the ship was due in Baltimore. It never showed. The Navy searched a million square miles of ocean. Found nothing. Not one plank. Not one body. President Wilson said only God and the sea knew what happened. Here's the part nobody talks about: twenty-three years later, two sister ships — same class, same manganese route — vanished too. Same silence. Same nothing. Three ships. One pattern. Zero answers, then or now.
