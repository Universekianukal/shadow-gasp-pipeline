February 11, 1979. A shallow grave on a coral atoll 2,300 miles from Maui. A cross made from driftwood. Inside: the remains of a man who disappeared ten years earlier. But that's getting ahead. 1979. Five men launch a 17-foot Boston Whaler from Maui — the Sarah Joe. A day fishing trip. They never come back. The search spans 50,000 square miles. Nothing. No wreckage, no bodies, no life vests. After ten days, the Coast Guard gives up. Five men swallowed by the Pacific. Ten years pass. 1989. A research vessel lands on Taongi Atoll — uninhabited, 2,300 miles southwest of Maui. Wreckage on the reef. The hull matches a Boston Whaler. The registration matches the Sarah Joe. A 17-foot open boat with no cabin, no GPS, no shade somehow crossed a quarter of the Pacific. Near the wreck: a shallow grave, a driftwood cross. Dental records identify Scott Moornan. One of the five. Then it gets stranger. Scattered near the grave: bones from a second person. Not one of the missing men. The bone structure suggests a Japanese fisherman. Someone else was on that island — someone who buried one of the crew and marked the grave. The other four men were never found. The second person was never identified. And nobody knows what happened on that atoll.
