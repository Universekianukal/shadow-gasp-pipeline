September 23rd, 1935. Two boys chase a rabbit down a gully in Kingsbury Run and find two headless bodies instead. One decapitated. Both emasculated. No blood at the scene — the killer drained them somewhere else first. Cleveland detectives call it murder number three. There will be nine more. Between 1935 and 1938, twelve victims are found across the same half-mile stretch of railroad tracks, dismembered with what doctors call 'surgical precision.' Only three are ever identified. Eliot Ness — the man who took down Al Capone — builds a task force. In August 1938 he burns down an entire shantytown in Kingsbury Run searching for the killer. He arrests no one there. But Ness has a suspect: Dr. Francis Sweeney, a surgeon, a WWI medic, who fails a lie detector test so badly the examiner calls it the worst result he's ever seen. Ness never charges him. Sweeney's cousin is a sitting congressman. Instead, police arrest a laborer named Frank Dolezal, beat a confession out of him. He dies in his cell with six broken ribs before trial. The murders just... stop. And for the next two decades, Sweeney mails Eliot Ness taunting postcards from the asylum where he's committed himself. Nobody ever answers what they meant.
